JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Format Should You Use?

Choosing the right image format can cut file sizes in half without any visible difference. Here is a plain-English comparison.

JPG (JPEG)

The classic photo format, supported everywhere. It uses lossy compression: it throws away detail your eyes are unlikely to notice. Excellent for photographs, poor for sharp lines and text, and it has no transparency.

PNG

A lossless format: every pixel is preserved exactly. Ideal for logos, interface screenshots and images with transparent backgrounds. For photographs, PNG files are often several times larger than JPG with no visible benefit.

WebP

A modern format that supports both lossy and lossless compression plus transparency. At comparable quality WebP files are typically smaller than JPG, which makes pages load faster. All current major browsers support it, but some older software and a few upload forms still only accept JPG or PNG.

Frequently asked questions

Is WebP better than JPG?

For websites, usually yes – smaller files at similar quality. For maximum compatibility with forms and older apps, JPG is safer.

Why is my PNG photo so big?

PNG is lossless, so it stores every detail of a photo. Convert it to JPG or WebP to shrink it.

What about HEIC?

HEIC is the default on iPhones. Many websites don't accept it, so convert to JPG before uploading.
