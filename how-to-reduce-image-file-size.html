How to Reduce Image File Size Without Losing Quality

Big photos slow down websites, bounce off upload forms and fill up email inboxes. These five techniques shrink images dramatically while keeping them looking sharp.

1. Resize to the size you actually need

Modern phones take photos 4000 pixels wide or more – far bigger than any screen shows. Halving the width cuts the number of pixels by three-quarters, and the file size falls with it. For web pages 1600–2000 px wide is plenty; for forms and profiles, 600–1000 px. Use the Max width option in our compressor or the image resizer.

2. Pick the right format

  • JPG – best all-round choice for photos.
  • WebP – usually smaller than JPG at the same quality; supported by all modern browsers.
  • PNG – for logos, screenshots and graphics with transparency; poor for photos.

More detail in JPG vs PNG vs WebP.

3. Lower the quality setting a little

JPG and WebP quality between 70% and 85% is usually indistinguishable from 100% on screen, but files are often half the size or less. Going below about 50% starts to show blocky artefacts, especially around edges and text.

4. Use a target size instead of guessing

If a form says “maximum 100 KB”, don't experiment with sliders. Choose the target size and let the tool find the best quality that fits automatically.

5. Crop out what you don't need

Empty sky, walls and borders still cost bytes. Cropping to the subject both improves the picture and shrinks the file.

Always keep your original. Compression is one-way: you can't get the lost detail back from the smaller file.

Frequently asked questions

What is lossless compression?

Compression that keeps every pixel identical, as in PNG. It saves less space than lossy formats like JPG and WebP.

Does compressing remove EXIF data?

Re-encoding creates a new file without the original camera metadata, including GPS location.

What is a good size for website images?

Under about 200 KB for large images and much less for thumbnails is a good target.
