Why Is My PDF So Large?

A ten-page PDF can be 200 KB or 50 MB depending on what's inside it. These are the usual culprits and how to fix each one.

1. Scanned pages

Each scanned page is a full-size photo. Scanning in colour at 300–600 DPI produces several megabytes per page. Fix: scan documents at 150–200 DPI in greyscale, or compress the PDF with our PDF compressor.

2. High-resolution photos

Photos pasted straight from a phone or camera keep their full resolution inside the PDF. Fix: compress images before inserting them, or use “reduce file size” export options.

3. Embedded fonts

PDFs embed fonts so they look the same everywhere. Many unusual fonts add up. Fix: use common fonts, and let your editor embed only the characters used (“subset” fonts).

4. Print-quality export settings

“High quality print” or “press quality” presets keep everything at maximum resolution. Fix: choose a “standard” or “minimum size” preset when exporting for screen or email.

5. Hidden extras

Layers, attachments, form data, editing history and thumbnails can all bloat a PDF. Re-saving or printing to a new PDF often strips them.

6. Repeatedly merged files

Combining many PDFs can duplicate resources such as fonts and images. Re-export the final document once instead of merging repeatedly.

Typed documents (mostly text) are usually already small. If compression makes little difference, the size is coming from fonts or settings rather than images.

Frequently asked questions

How big should a normal PDF be?

A text document is often under 100 KB per page; scanned pages are commonly 100 KB–1 MB each.

Does compression delete pages?

No. All pages are kept.

Can I make a PDF smaller without losing quality?

Partly – removing unused data and fonts is lossless; reducing image quality saves the most space.
