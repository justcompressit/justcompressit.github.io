How to Compress a PDF for Email

“Attachment too large” is one of the most common email errors. Here is how to get a PDF small enough to send – and what to do if it's still too big.

Typical email size limits

Most major email services cap a single message at roughly 20–25 MB including all attachments, and the recipient's provider may accept less. Because attachments are encoded for sending, the real limit for your files is a little lower than the number advertised. Aim to keep attachments well under 10 MB to be safe.

Compress the PDF in three steps

  1. Open the PDF compressor and choose your file.
  2. Pick a target such as Under 2 MB or the “Recommended” level.
  3. Download the smaller copy and attach it.

If it's still too large

  • Choose Black & white for documents – often halves the size again.
  • Send it in parts – split chapters or sections into separate emails.
  • Share a link – upload to a cloud drive and email the link instead.
  • Re-export from the source – Word, PowerPoint and Google Docs can export PDFs with compressed images, keeping text selectable.

Why some PDFs are huge

Usually it's scanned pages or high-resolution photos inside the file. See why is my PDF so large?

Frequently asked questions

What size PDF can I email?

Keep it under 10 MB for reliable delivery; most services reject messages over about 20–25 MB.

Is it safe to compress confidential PDFs online?

With JustCompressIt the file never leaves your device. Many other online tools do upload files to their servers.

Will the recipient notice lower quality?

For scans and photos at the recommended level, usually not.
